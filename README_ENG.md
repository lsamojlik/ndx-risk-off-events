**Problem Description**:

This analysis examined which stocks are better opportunities during a market correction/negative sentiment. It studied how Nasdaq-100 stocks (ticker NDX) performed 1 month, 3 months, and 12 months later, on days when at least 75% of Nasdaq-100 stocks (the Nasdaq-100 was chosen instead of S&P 500 to avoid sector rotation within the index) closed down. The analysis aimed to answer whether stocks that resisted the sentiment signal strong fundamentals that the market was unwilling to sell, or whether it is better to look for significantly oversold stocks. It also verified whether buying oversold stocks during broad market corrections is a good idea, assuming negative sentiment affects the entire market, where a stock “takes a hit” but its fundamentals often remain unchanged.

**Data Sources**:

• Current NASDAQ-100 composition and index changes from 01.02.2007 to the date of the study: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasdaq-100

• yfinance library (Yahoo Finance)
  
**Research Assumptions**:

• Study period: 01.02.2007–19.12.2025

• Horizon for future return analysis: 1M (21 trading days ahead), 3M (63 trading days ahead), 12M (252 trading days ahead)

• Only stocks that were part of the NASDAQ-100 on a given day were considered (excluding delisted stocks – Yahoo Finance does not provide data for delisted companies).

• A “broad” correction was defined as a drop of at least 75% of NASDAQ-100 stocks (based on the difference between the current day’s Adjusted Close price and the previous day’s Adjusted Close). In the studied period, there were 696 such corrections.
________________________________________

**Summary and Investor Insights**:

**1. “Buy stocks falling with the index” strategy (trend down) vs. “Buy stocks resilient to declines” strategy (trend against)**
    
• Historically, buying stocks falling together with the index during broad corrections produced higher average and median returns over 1M, 3M, and 12M horizons.

• Standard deviation of returns was slightly lower, indicating less risk of deviation from the mean return compared to buying “resilient” stocks.


**2. Extreme situations (≥95% of stocks falling)**
   
• Even on such days, most analyzed stocks showed positive returns in the following 1M, 3M, and 12M periods.

• This indicates that extreme corrections present attractive opportunities to buy stocks at lower prices.


**3. Comparison with random NASDAQ-100 index purchases**
   
• Buying during broad correction days (trend down strategy) produced higher average returns than randomly purchasing the index, though with greater variability.

• This suggests that broad correction moments are more effective for stock selection and potentially generating above-average returns compared to investing in the index on a random day.


**4. Key takeaway for investors – using broad corrections as an investment opportunity**
   
• A broad correction affecting many unrelated stocks creates the best investment opportunities.

• The ability to identify which stocks truly reflect fundamental risk versus those responding only to market sentiment provides a competitive edge and the potential to outperform the index.
